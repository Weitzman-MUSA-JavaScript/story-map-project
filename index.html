Philadelphia's Rising Waterfront

Exploring how six sea-level rise scenarios could transform Philadelphia's Delaware River waterfront.

Scroll to examine potential inundation from 1 to 6 feet above Mean Higher High Water.

1 Foot Above MHHW

At one foot above Mean Higher High Water, inundation begins spreading across low-lying areas along Philadelphia's Deleware and Schuylkill River edges.

This is a water-level scenario rather than a prodiction for specific year.

2 Foot Above MHHW

At two feet, inundation spreads beyond the immediate river edge and reaches more low-lying land along the Delaware and Schuylkill Rivers.

3 Foot Above MHHW

At three feet, previously separated areas of inundation begin to connect, creating a more continuous zone of exposure along the waterfront.

4 Foot Above MHHW

At four feet, inundation extends farther inland and affects a larger portion of Philadelphia's waterfront, including industrial land, infrastructure, and riverfront open spaces.

5 Feet Above MHHW

At five feet, Inundation expands substantially across low-lying waterfront areas and infrastructure along both rivers.

6 Foot Above MHHW

At six feet, the inundation footprint reaches its greatest extent in this story, substantially transforming the relationship between Philadelphia and its rivers.

Planning for a Changing Waterfront

The six scenarios show that sea-level rise would not affect the waterfront all at once. Exposure expands gradually, but each additional foot places more land, infrastructure, and public space within the potential inundation area.

These maps can help identify where adaptation strategies including wetlands, floodable parks, elevated infrastructure, and protected evacuation routes may be most important.
